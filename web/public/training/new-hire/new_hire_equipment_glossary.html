Equipment & Terminology

Every piece of equipment and every term used across the HVAC, Life Safety, Plumbing, Electrical, and BMS overviews — in one place. Keep it in the toolbox for the first few weeks.

01

HVAC

Equipment

AHUAir Handling UnitFilter, coils, fan in one casing. Conditions the air and pushes it out. Sections: OA damper → ERM coil → filter → CHW coil → HHW coil → fan.
ChillerMakes chilled water. Moves the building's heat into the condenser water.
Cooling TowerRejects that heat to the atmosphere by evaporation.
BoilerMakes hot water. Condensing (~120–140°F), non-condensing (~160–180°F), or a steam-to-water HX fed by campus steam.
Free-Cooling HX & PumpHeat ExchangerWhen it's cold out, tower water chills the CHW loop directly — compressors off.
ERMEnergy Recovery ModuleTwo coils, one in the OA stream (inside the AHU) and one in the exhaust duct, joined by a pumped glycol run-around loop. Preheats in winter, pre-cools in summer. Airstreams never touch.
LEFLab Exhaust FanThrows lab exhaust high above the roof. On emergency power.
GEFGeneral Exhaust FanPulls restrooms, electrical/MDF, elevator machine and mechanical rooms straight to the roof. No ERM, no LEF.
UH / CUHUnit Heater / Cabinet Unit HeaterLocal heater for docks, vestibules, stairs, garages, mech rooms. Often there for freeze protection.
VAVVariable Air Volume boxDamper + airflow sensor. No cooling coil — it cools by admitting more cold air. Reheat coil on a call for heat.
FPTFan Powered TerminalVAV plus its own fan, pulling return air through a return duct/plenum. Reheat coil; four-pipe units add a CHW coil.
CRAC / CRAHComputer Room Air Conditioner / Air HandlerRecirculates the room's own air across a coil, 24/7. CRAH = chilled water (ours); CRAC = built-in DX compressor.
Exhaust valveventuri valve / damperMeters air out of a room. What makes a lab negative.
WSHPWater-Source Heat PumpZone unit rejecting/absorbing heat on a shared water loop.
HV / MAU (DOAS)Heating & Ventilating / Make-Up Air UnitBrings fresh air to a WSHP building; exhaust takes it out.
PumpsCW · CHW · HW · loop pumpsNo pump, no flow, no cooling or heat.
OA louver · OA damperOutside AirLouver is in the wall; damper is the AHU's first section.
EconomizerOffice AHUs only: open the OA dampers wide in mild weather and cool with fresh air. A 100% OA lab AHU has no use for one.

Terminology

H / V / ACHeating · Ventilation · Air Conditioning
OAOutside Air
CFMCubic Feet per MinuteHow much air is moving.
ACHAir Changes per HourCFM = ACH × room volume ÷ 60. Office 4–6; lab 6–12; setback 2–6.
CHW / HHW / CWChilled water / Heating hot water / Condenser water
DXDirect expansionRefrigerant coil, no chilled water.
Freezestatlow-limitA hardwired, manual-reset switch that senses the coldest foot of its capillary, not the average. It stops the fan and closes the OA damper. The BMS is informed; it is not asked.
High-static cutoutsupply ductA hardwired, manual-reset pressure switch that stops the fan before the duct bursts. Cause: a shut fire damper, a stuck damper, or every VAV driving closed.
Low-static cutoutreturn / exhaust ductA hardwired, manual-reset switch that stops the fan before the duct implodes. Ducts are rated for far less negative than positive pressure, and buckling is sudden and total.
Shutoff headfan curveThe pressure a fan makes at zero flow — often 6–10" w.c. on an AHU, well past the duct’s rating. A VFD fan is a pressure source, not a flow source: close the path and it keeps pushing.
Fan status proofcurrent switch · DP switchIndependent proof the fan is really moving air. Nothing in the start sequence is released to control until status makes. Command is not proof.
Access-door switchAHU door interlockA hardwired permissive: open a door and the fan drops. It exists because people go inside these boxes — and it is not a substitute for LOTO.
Reheat penaltyAir cooled to ~55°F, then warmed back up at the terminal. You paid twice.
Free coolingTower + HX chills the loop; compressors rest.
Outdoor resetSupply setpoint slides with the weather.
Mass balancesupply + infiltration = exhaust + exfiltration.
Building pressureHeld +0.02 to +0.05 in. w.c. positive to outside.
Pressure cascadeAir flows clean → dirty. Labs negative, corridors positive, restrooms negative.
Tracking pairSupply valve follows the exhaust valve to hold the offset.
Offset CFMHow a room is held negative — not a pressure knob.
Stack effect · windCold day/tall building: lower floors negative, upper positive; wind moves the neutral plane.
Sensible / latent / total heat1.08 × CFM × ΔT · 0.68 × CFM × Δgrains · 4.5 × CFM × Δh
Sash managementClosing sashes lets airflow fall back to the ACH floor. An energy program.
MDF roomMain Distribution FrameIT room; heat, not fumes — served by the GEF.

Equipment

NotificationHorns, strobes, voice communication.

Fire pumpBoosts pressure so sprinklers and standpipes get real flow.

StandpipeFire department hose connections.
Smoke / fire dampersClose on alarm to compartmentalize.
Stair pressurization fansPush air into the stairs so smoke stays out of the escape path.
Elevator recallCars return to a safe floor and stop serving the alarmed zone.
Door hold-open / releaseMagnetically held doors drop shut on alarm.
Generator & ATSAutomatic Transfer SwitchTwo inputs, one output. Life-safety loads transfer first.
Gas detection & shutoffLab layer.
Emergency showers & eyewashTepid water, tested regularly.

Plumbing

Equipment

RPZReduced Pressure Zone assemblyHighest level of backflow protection. Two checks in series, relief valve between them. Field names: RP, “the backflow.” Tested annually.
Water meter & serviceMeasurement and the main shutoff.
PRVPressure-Reducing ValveSteps street pressure down to a steady, safe level.
Domestic booster pumpPressure for the upper floors.
Water heater & recirc loopStore ~140°F, deliver ~120°F through a mixing valve.
Mixing valveBlends cold in to temper the delivered hot water.
P-trapA U of standing water sealing the drain against sewer gas.
Vent stackLets air in and sewer gas out so drains flow and traps hold their seal.
CleanoutCapped access stub at the base of the stack.
Sump / ejector pumpLifts below-grade waste up to the sewer.
Roof drain & overflowRain leaders to the storm system — separate piping.
Oil / water separatorGarage and loading-dock drains. Oil floats and is pumped out; only water goes to sanitary.
Neutralization tankCorrects lab-waste pH before the sewer.
RO / DI skidReverse Osmosis / DeionizationFed by potable through its own RPZ. Output is lab process water on dedicated piping.
Cooling tower make-upReclaimed, or potable through PRV + RPZ.

Terminology

Potable / non-potableTwo systems, never cross-connected. Non-potable pipe is marked and labeled.
Cross-connection · backflowThe biggest plumbing risk in a lab building.
DHWDomestic Hot WaterStored 140°F (Legionella), delivered 120°F (scald).
Legionella / scaldThe two competing risks the mixing valve and recirc loop manage.
Water hammerThe bang when fast-moving water is stopped suddenly by a closing valve.
pHAcid/base scale 0–14, 7 neutral. Sewer-safe band roughly 6–9.
Resistivity / conductivityMΩ·cm / µS/cmRO/DI purity. Tap ~300–800 µS/cm · RO ~5–50 · DI <0.1 ≈ 18.2 MΩ·cm. Falling MΩ = spent resin.
Storm vs sanitaryRain and only rain → storm. Touched anything else → sanitary (with treatment on the way if needed).
BlowdownTower bleed carries treatment chemicals → sanitary, never storm.
WSHP condenser loopWater-Source Heat PumpSmall potable make-up, protected by an RPZ.

Electrical

Equipment

Utility service & transformerMedium voltage stepped down to 480V, the building's backbone.
Main switchgearMain breaker, feeder breakers, metering, bus duct.
Panels & step-down transformers480V → 277V lighting; dry-type transformers make 208/120V for receptacles.
MCCMotor Control CenterStarters, overloads, and disconnects for pumps and fans in one lineup.
VFDVariable Frequency DriveVaries motor speed so pumps and fans sip power at part load.
GeneratorBuilds the emergency bus in roughly ten seconds. Weekly test.
ATSAutomatic Transfer SwitchTwo inputs, one output. Life-safety transfers first, standby follows.
Emergency busWhat the generator feeds; the ATSs hang off it.
UPSUninterruptible Power SupplyBatteries bridge the ten-second gap for loads that cannot blink.
Breakersmain → feeder → branchEach protects the wiring downstream of it.
Red outletsGenerator-backed standby circuits. −80 freezers plug in here.
No-load exerciseweekly testThe generator runs, the ATS never transfers, and the building notices nothing. That is the test working — and the reason people misread a running generator.
Cool-downunloaded run before shutdownA hot turbocharged diesel must run unloaded before it stops, or oil cokes in the turbo bearing. It will not fail today. It will fail on the night you need it.
E-stop lockoutPressing the generator E-stop puts the set in lockout until somebody resets it by hand. A generator that merely looked wrong becomes genuinely unavailable.
EPOEmergency Power OffKills a lab's power instantly. Know where they are.

Terminology

Normal / emergency / standby / criticalThe four tiers of power.
Retransfer & cool-downUtility returns, ATSs retransfer on a delay, generator runs unloaded then stops.
Arc flashThe label states the hazard and required PPE. Hands off energized gear until trained and authorized.
LOTOLockout / TagoutLock, tag, then verify dead — live-dead-live.
Reset-once ruleA trip is information. Investigate, reset once, never hold a breaker in against a fault.
BMSBuilding Management SystemSits behind the UPS.
05

BMS

Equipment

Front end / workstationserver + graphicsWhere you look, not where control happens. Graphics, trends, alarms, schedules. Pull it off the network and the controllers keep controlling.
Supervisory controllernetwork controllerHolds schedules, reset schedules, global setpoints, trend storage, alarm logic. Most of a BMS’s energy savings live here.
Field controllerDDC / unitary controllerOne small computer per piece of equipment — an AHU, a plant, every VAV box. Runs its own loops and keeps running them if the network goes quiet.
SensorsinputsTemperature, humidity, DP, airflow, CO₂, and status contacts — current switches and aux contacts that prove a thing is really running.
ActuatorsoutputsValve and damper actuators, VFD speed commands, start/stop relays. The only parts that move.
PointsAI · AO · BI · BOAnalog input (a temperature), analog output (a valve), binary input (a status), binary output (a start command). Virtual points hold calculated values.
BACnetMS/TP · IPThe common language. MS/TP on a twisted-pair field bus; BACnet/IP over the building network. Modbus on chillers and meters, LON on older gear.
Trends & alarmsA trend is a point sampled over time; an alarm is a limit crossed. Together they turn a snapshot into a story.
Schedulesoccupancy · optimal startOccupied/unoccupied modes, holidays, and starting early enough to hit setpoint at occupancy — no earlier.
Trend loghistoryA point recorded over time, on an interval or on change of value (COV). You cannot trend the past — if a point was never logged, that history does not exist.
Field busMS/TP trunkThe twisted-pair daisy chain the field controllers sit on. A whole branch of the graphic going stale at once points at the bus, not at a device.

Terminology

BMS / BASBuilding Management / Automation SystemThe whole thing: controllers, network, and front end.
DDCDirect Digital ControlControl done by a computer rather than a pneumatic or mechanical device.
Setpoint vs process variableSP / PVSetpoint is what you want; PV is what you have. Their difference is the error. Never report a reading without its setpoint beside it.
PIDProportional · Integral · DerivativeP reacts to how big the error is now, I to how long it has persisted, D to how fast it changes. Most HVAC loops use P and I.
DeadbandA band around setpoint where nothing is commanded — it stops heating and cooling fighting each other and equipment short-cycling.
HuntingA valve swinging open and shut, never settling. Too much gain, or integral wind-up. A tuning fault, not a mechanical one.
Command vs statusCommand is what the BMS asked for. Status is independent proof. Command ON with status OFF is a fault — and the status point is only as good as the device behind it.
Saturated outputpinned at 100% or 0%A modulating device that never moves. The loop is asking for everything it has and still missing setpoint. It almost never alarms.
Stale pointA value that stopped updating: comm loss, dead sensor, or an override. Real sensors wiggle — never trust a flat trend line.
Interlock / permissiveA condition proven before equipment may run — flow proven before a chiller starts. Satisfy it; never defeat it.
Override / HandA point forced by a person. Schedules, resets, and alarms are all bypassed. An override is a promise to come back.
Reset scheduleA setpoint that slides with a measured condition — hot water hotter as it gets colder out; DP setpoint down when valves are mostly closed. The biggest energy lever the BMS has.
Cascade controlOne loop’s output becomes another loop’s setpoint. A slow room loop steers a fast supply-air loop.
Fail positionnormally open / closedWhere a spring-return actuator goes on power loss. OA dampers usually fail closed to protect coils from freezing.
Alarm fatigueWhen everything alarms, nothing does. A nuisance alarm is a defect — fix the cause, or retune the limit deliberately and write down why.
Hardware pointphysical · AI AO BI BOLanded on a controller terminal, wired to a real device, occupying an I/O slot. When it is wrong, the fault is in the field — sensor, wiring, transmitter, calibration, linkage.
Software pointvirtual · AV BVLives only in the controller’s memory: setpoints, alarm limits, occupancy flags, calculated values, runtime totals. When it is wrong, the fault is in the logic. Nothing in the field will help you.
Network pointmapped / integratedRead from another device over BACnet or Modbus — chiller amps, meter kWh, VFD reported speed. When it is wrong, the fault is in the integration: gateway, register map, address, or the third-party device.
Status echothe trapA “status” that is really a software point mirroring the command. It turns green because the BMS asked, not because anything happened — so it can never reveal a failure. If command and status have never once disagreed, look for a wire.
Process variablePV — a role, not a point typeThe reading a particular loop is controlling to. Its partner is the setpoint; a status’s partner is the command. Usually an input, never a status — and it can be calculated, arrive over the network, or be binary.
Present_ValueBACnet propertyA property every BACnet object has — AI, AO, BI, BO, AV. An AO’s Present_Value is the command it is sending, not a measurement. “Present value” in a point list is not “process variable” in a loop, though both get shortened to PV.
COVChange Of ValueA trend that logs only when the value moves, rather than on a timer. A five-minute interval will not show a valve hunting on a thirty-second cycle.
Sequence of operationSOOThe document that says what the building is supposed to do. Every setpoint and timer defers to it.

Where the disciplines meet

BMS ↔ everythingthe nervous systemEvery discipline on this sheet reports to, or is commanded by, the BMS — except the one that must not be.
BMS ↔ Life Safetythe boundaryThe FACP is stand-alone. It commands dampers, fans, and elevator recall through hardwired paths; the BMS may only monitor. A hardwired interlock always beats a software command.
BMS ↔ ElectricalUPSThe BMS rides the UPS so it never sees the ten-second gap before the generator picks up.
AHU · ERM · LEF · GEFHVAC ↔ Life SafetyLab exhaust is life safety: the LEF rides emergency power and fails safe.
ATS · GeneratorLife Safety ↔ ElectricalSame sequence taught on both pages: sense → start → build bus → transfer, life safety first.
Cooling towerHVAC ↔ PlumbingMake-up water in (potable + RPZ, or reclaimed); blowdown out to sanitary.
WSHPHVAC ↔ PlumbingCondenser loop make-up is potable through an RPZ.
MDF / electrical roomsHVAC ↔ ElectricalServed by the GEF for heat removal. A dead GEF throttles or trips equipment.
RO / DIPlumbing ↔ lab layerPotable + RPZ in; ultra-pure process water out on its own piping.
BMSElectrical ↔ Life SafetyOn the UPS, so it never sees the transfer gap.
Numbers quoted here (44°F chilled water; 140–180°F hot water; 6–12 ACH; +0.02–0.05 in. w.c.) are typical ballparks to build intuition. Always defer to the actual setpoints, sequences of operation, and tenant EH&S requirements for the building you are standing in.
